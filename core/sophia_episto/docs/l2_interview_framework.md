# L2 Knowledge Elicitation Interview

## Purpose

This interview is designed to extract your tacit trading knowledge and convert it into explicit L2 frameworks. The goal is not comprehensive coverage on first pass - it's to capture the core mental models you actually use when making decisions, in enough detail that an agent could retrieve and apply them.

For each section, answer what feels natural. Skip what doesn't resonate. We'll iterate.

---

## Part 1: Framework Decomposition

*For each domain, we want to understand how you break down the problem.*

### 1.1 Term Premium

1. How do you decompose term premium? What are the component drivers you think about?

2. Which of these drivers are slow-moving (regime-level) versus fast-moving (tactical)?

3. When you say "term premium is rich" or "term premium is cheap," what are you comparing it to? What's your reference point or fair value anchor?

4. What's the relationship between term premium and duration supply? How do you think about that causality?

5. Are there quantitative levels or thresholds that matter to you, or is it more about direction and momentum?

6. **Tell me about a time when** your read on term premium was clearly right - you saw something others missed. What did you see and why did you trust it?

7. **Tell me about a time when** term premium moved in a way that surprised you. What happened, and did it change how you think about it?

---

### 1.2 Positioning & Flows

1. How do you think about positioning? What data or signals do you use to assess it?

2. What's your mental model of who the main players are in rates markets and how they behave differently?

3. When does positioning matter for price action versus when is it noise?

4. How do you distinguish between "crowded" and "consensual but not crowded"?

5. What flow dynamics do you watch? Are there predictable calendar effects or institutional patterns you rely on?

6. **Tell me about a time when** you correctly identified that positioning was offsides before a move. What tipped you off?

7. **Tell me about a time when** you underestimated how crowded a trade was. What did the unwind look like and what did you learn?

---

### 1.3 Fed Reaction Function

1. How do you model what the Fed will do? What variables drive their decisions in your mental model?

2. How do you weight what they say versus what they do? When does communication matter more than action?

3. What's your framework for Fed credibility? How do you know when the market believes them versus doesn't?

4. How do you think about the asymmetry in their reaction function - do they respond differently to upside versus downside surprises?

5. How has your model of the Fed changed over time? What regime shifts have you lived through that updated your priors?

6. **Tell me about a time when** you correctly anticipated a Fed pivot before consensus. What signaled the shift?

7. **Tell me about a time when** Fed communication wrong-footed you. What did you miss in the messaging?

8. **Tell me about a time when** the market's interpretation of a Fed event differed from yours. Who was right, and what did that teach you?

---

### 1.4 Supply Dynamics

1. How do you think about Treasury supply? What's your framework for when supply matters versus when it's absorbed without impact?

2. What's the relationship between supply, term premium, and auction outcomes in your mental model?

3. How do you think about the demand side - who are the marginal buyers at different points in the cycle?

4. What supply-related signals have predictive value for you?

5. **Tell me about a time when** supply dynamics drove a move that the macro narrative missed. What happened?

6. **Tell me about a time when** you expected supply to matter and it didn't. What absorbed it?

---

### 1.5 Macro Regime

1. How do you define the current macro regime? What are the key state variables?

2. What tells you a regime is shifting versus experiencing noise within a stable regime?

3. How do you think about the interaction between growth, inflation, and policy? Is there a hierarchy?

4. What's your framework for fiscal-monetary interaction? When do fiscal dynamics dominate?

5. **Tell me about a time when** you correctly identified a regime shift early. What were the early signals?

6. **Tell me about a time when** you thought the regime was shifting but it was just noise. What fooled you?

---

## Part 2: Decision Rules & Heuristics

*Beyond frameworks, you have rules of thumb. These are often the highest-signal content for L2.*

### 2.1 Conviction Formation

1. What combination of signals gives you high conviction? Is there a pattern?

2. What makes you override your base case? What type of new information changes your mind?

3. How do you distinguish between "I'm early" and "I'm wrong"?

4. What's your process when multiple frameworks point in different directions?

5. **Tell me about a time when** you had unusually high conviction and it paid off. What gave you that confidence?

6. **Tell me about a time when** you had high conviction and it was wrong. What was the error - the analysis or the expression?

7. **Tell me about a time when** you correctly changed your mind mid-trade. What triggered the update?

---

### 2.2 Trade Expression

1. How do you choose between outright duration, curve, and RV expressions?

2. What makes a trade "clean" versus "messy" in your mind?

3. When do you prefer optionality versus delta? What drives that choice?

4. How do you think about carry versus roll versus capital appreciation?

5. **Tell me about a time when** you had the right view but the wrong expression. What would have been better?

6. **Tell me about a time when** the expression saved you despite an imperfect view. What made it resilient?

---

### 2.3 Risk & Sizing

1. How do you size positions relative to conviction? Is there a rough mapping?

2. What makes you cut a position before your thesis is invalidated?

3. How do you think about correlated risks across positions?

4. What's your mental model of liquidity - when does it matter and when can you ignore it?

5. **Tell me about a time when** you sized correctly for uncertainty - stayed small enough to survive being wrong. What made you hold back?

6. **Tell me about a time when** you wished you'd sized bigger. What stopped you?

7. **Tell me about a time when** liquidity mattered more than you expected. What happened?

---

## Part 3: Failure Modes & Lessons

*Where your frameworks have broken down is often more informative than where they've worked.*

### 3.1 Historical Misses

1. What's a trade or view that was wrong in a way that taught you something durable?

2. What framework failed, and how did you update it?

3. Are there market conditions where your usual approach doesn't work? What are the signatures of those conditions?

4. **Tell me about** your most painful loss that wasn't due to bad luck. What was the actual mistake?

5. **Tell me about** a time you were right for the wrong reasons. Did you realize it at the time?

---

### 3.2 Known Limitations

1. What aspects of rates markets do you feel you understand well versus poorly?

2. What types of moves consistently surprise you?

3. Where do you know you have blind spots?

---

### 3.3 Epistemic Pitfalls

1. When do you tend to hold a view too long? What's the pattern?

2. When do you tend to give up too early?

3. What emotional or cognitive states lead to your worst decisions?

4. **Tell me about a time when** you knew you were in a bad mental state for trading but traded anyway. What happened?

5. **Tell me about a time when** you caught yourself rationalizing a position. What snapped you out of it?

---

## Part 4: Boundaries of Applicability

*Frameworks work in context. We need to capture when they apply and when they don't.*

### 4.1 Regime Dependence

1. Which of your frameworks are regime-dependent? What defines the applicable regime?

2. How do you know when you've shifted into a regime where your playbook doesn't apply?

3. What's an example of a framework that worked in one regime and failed in another?

4. **Tell me about** the hardest regime transition you've traded through. What made it hard and how did you adapt?

---

### 4.2 Cross-Market Interactions

1. When do cross-market signals (equities, credit, FX, commodities) matter for rates?

2. How do you think about correlation regime shifts?

3. What cross-market dynamics do you watch as leading indicators?

4. **Tell me about a time when** a cross-market signal gave you edge in rates. What was the connection?

5. **Tell me about a time when** correlations broke down on you. What was the shock?

---

### 4.3 Scope Limits

1. What parts of the rates market do you explicitly avoid or feel underqualified to trade?

2. What types of analysis do you rely on others for?

3. Where does your edge end?

---

## Part 5: Meta-Frameworks

*How you think about thinking.*

### 5.1 Information Processing

1. What sources do you trust most? Why?

2. How do you distinguish signal from noise in research?

3. What makes you update a view based on research versus dismiss it as noise?

4. **Tell me about a time when** a piece of research genuinely changed your view. What made it credible?

5. **Tell me about a time when** you initially dismissed something that turned out to be important. What did you learn about your filters?

---

### 5.2 Market Epistemics

1. When is consensus informative versus when is it something to fade?

2. How do you think about what's priced in versus what isn't?

3. What's your model of how information gets incorporated into rates markets?

4. **Tell me about a time when** you correctly faded consensus. What told you the crowd was wrong?

5. **Tell me about a time when** you faded consensus and got run over. What did consensus know that you didn't?

---

### 5.3 Self-Calibration

1. How do you know when you're calibrated versus overconfident or underconfident?

2. What feedback loops do you use to assess your own accuracy?

3. How do you distinguish skill from luck in your own performance?

4. **Tell me about** a period when you were running hot. How did you know whether it was skill or conditions?

5. **Tell me about** a period when nothing worked. How did you diagnose whether it was you or the market?

---

## Instructions for Use

1. Work through these questions at whatever pace feels right. You don't need to answer all of them in one sitting.

2. Answers can be brief or detailed - what matters is capturing the actual model you use, not performing comprehensiveness.

3. If a question doesn't resonate or doesn't match how you think, skip it or reframe it. The interview should adapt to your cognition, not the other way around.

4. Contradictions are fine and even valuable - they reveal where your frameworks have tension.

5. After we have a first pass, I'll help structure the answers into YAML format for your L2 domain model.

---

## Output Format (for eventual L2 conversion)

Each framework will eventually be structured as:

```yaml
framework:
  name: [descriptive name]
  domain: [rates | macro | positioning | etc.]
  decomposition:
    - [component 1]
    - [component 2]
  key_relationships:
    - [relationship 1]
    - [relationship 2]
  signals:
    - signal: [what you watch]
      interpretation: [what it means]
  decision_rules:
    - condition: [when]
      action: [then]
  failure_modes:
    - [when this breaks down]
  regime_applicability:
    - [when this applies]
  confidence: [high | medium | low]
  last_updated: [date]
  provenance: [source - in this case, "Nate interview"]
```
